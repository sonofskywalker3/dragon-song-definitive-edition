# Japanese Lunar leaves the first Dragonmaster unwritten

The Japanese Lunar games establish far less about Lunar's deep past than English-language fan lore suggests, and that thinness is the rewrite's biggest asset. Japanese Lunar 1 calls the migration from the Blue Star a 伝説 (legend) with no date, method, or passenger list; Japanese Lunar 2 (checked first-hand against the Saturn disc's voiced Goddess Tower movies, see the correction below) has Zophar's war ruin the Blue Star, Althena and Lucia "barely" seal him, and the Blue Star fall into a long sleep; the Japanese Wikipedia claim that Althena herself destroyed the Blue Star as the price of the seal is not in the game. No Japanese text explains where beastmen (獣人) came from, and the Vile Tribe (魔族) are already a non-human race in the 1992 original (horned, winged, black-bodied, counted with 匹, C009.MAP @0x2520); what the 1996 remake changed is the Frontier's *villagers*, ordinary humans and beastmen in 1992 (Cadin, Marke, Talon, Ruid), who are gone in 1996 (superseded 2026-10-10; see the supersessions list below). **Corrected 2026-10-09 from the ripped Japanese Mega CD discs:** the Japanese Mega CD Eternal Blue history book names "the first Dragonmaster, Louie (ルイ), who descended to this land from the Blue Star together with the goddess Althena" (M0A.GRP @0x1BA78), so a Dragonmaster did cross with her and is canon, and the Japanese Mega CD Silver Star library says the dead world "was changed to a green land by the first Dragonmaster told of in myth" (C021.MAP @0x5226) and that "500 years ago" Althena "exiled the Vile Tribe to the Frontier and sealed them" (C021.MAP @0x265D): the 500-year figure and the seal are in the 1992 original (the Saturn remake dropped both). For Ignatius, making him a Dragonmaster later in an already-old line contradicts nothing in Japanese Lunar 1 or 2 and matches the template Game Arts itself used in 2009 for Harmony's Aifeln (a former companion of Althena and the Dragonmaster who turns on them over the nature of gods). Making him the Dragonmaster who came with Althena imports Lunar 2's heaviest lore into a game that otherwise ignores it, fights Dragon Song's own line that he "took" the White Dragon's pre-existing trial, and forces the writer to specify what Game Arts deliberately left as legend. Dragon Song's genuine contradictions with Lunar 1 and 2 are elsewhere: Althena abdicating and being reborn a thousand years early, a Vile Tribe nation existing some 500 years before Lunar 1 says it was exiled, and Ignatius being credited as that tribe's maker. On sourcing, every Lunar 1 and 2 disc in both languages, both spinoffs, Lunar Legend's strings, and Harmony's English (including the Four Heroes prologue, TEXT060.dat) are now dumped under `lunar_scripts/` (see its README); the sourcing section below is kept as history.

**How to read the labels.** Every claim below carries one tag. **[JP]** is Japanese game text or a Japanese official or press source (Japanese Wikipedia, Famitsu, Nintendo, Game Arts) quoted or paraphrased. **[JP via fan]** is Japanese game text known only through a fan translator's English rendering (Kizyr of LunarNET, reposted in the LP Archive); the Japanese wording has not been seen. **[EN-WD]** is Working Designs' English script (Sega CD or PS1). **[EN-other]** is another English layer: Ubisoft (Legend, Dragon Song), XSEED (Harmony), or the Aeon Genesis fan patch (Walking School). **[FAN]** is English fan-wiki, forum, or blog synthesis without citation. **[DS script]** is this project's own dump of Dragon Song's USA and Japanese text (`docs/story-characters.md`, message ids in parentheses). **[INFERENCE]** is the author's reading. Every Japanese in-game quotation from Lunar 1 and 2 in this report is secondhand: no Japanese script of any Lunar game exists online, and the research could not rip one. The gaps section of each topic says what would settle it.

## Japanese Lunar 1 calls the migration a legend; Japanese Lunar 2 has Zophar's war ruin the Blue Star and the seal barely hold

**First-hand corrections (2026-10-09), from the ripped Japanese Mega CD discs (lunar_scripts/tss_segacd_jp, eb_segacd_jp):**

- **[JP]** Silver Star (1992), Vane library, C021.MAP @0x5226: 「かつて この世界は 生命のない 死の世界であった だが 神話で語られる初代の ドラゴンマスターによって 緑の大地に変えられたという」 (the dead world was changed into a green land by the first Dragonmaster told of in myth). C021.MAP @0x265D: 「５００年前 この世界には 人々を苦しめる 魔族たちが住んでいた‥‥ しかし 女神アルテナは 人々のために 魔族を 辺境に追放して 封印したのだ」 (500 years ago the Vile Tribe that tormented people lived in this world; Althena exiled them to the Frontier and sealed them). The Saturn Silver Star Story has neither the number nor 封印; it says only 追放.
- **[JP]** Eternal Blue (1994), Vane library, M0A.GRP @0x1BA78: 「女神アルテナとともに 青き星よりこの地に降り立った 初代ドラゴンマスター・ルイ」 (the first Dragonmaster, Louie, who descended to this land from the Blue Star together with the goddess Althena). So "Louie" is canon, not a fan-wiki invention as stated below. M0A.GRP @0x1BB20 lists 疾風ゼオン, 鉄拳ロカ, 金と銀のアスティ・リーザ姉妹, 黒竜戦役の四英雄ダイン, 最後のドラゴンマスター アレス (the Saturn version renames the sisters アリシア・リィナ). The Goddess Tower recording is voiced on the Mega CD too; the Saturn transcript stands as the Japanese wording.
- Every statement below that calls Louie or the first Dragonmaster "fan-wiki only" or "reachable only through a fan's rendering" is superseded by these lines.

**Supersessions (2026-10-10), from the full canon report (`reports/Lunar canon timeline and Dragon Song fixes.md`, gitignored, local-only; notes in `research_notes/Lunar canon timeline and Dragon Song fixes/`).** Where the body below still says otherwise, these first-hand lines win:

- **Louie is canon** (M0A.GRP @0x1BA78; Saturn L2 1574 @0x3060; WD PS1 "Louie"). The body's "Do not adopt Louie" is withdrawn; the bible (11a) names him. Louie came with Althena; who greened the world differs by version (1992: the first Dragonmaster, C021.MAP @0x5226; 1996: Althena, TEXT016.DAT @0x9596), so Retold should not say who did.
- **Althena did not destroy the Blue Star.** O064 and O065 (`lunar_scripts/l2eb_saturn_jp/goddess_tower_jp.txt`): wounded (傷つき), barely sealed (かろうじて); the only cost is the Blue Dragon's silenced line (Saturn L2 1483 @0xB8E). The ja.wikipedia "destroyed in exchange for the seal" claim and the "burned her first world" inference below are withdrawn.
- **"Althena's light does not reach the Frontier" is Japanese** (TSS-JP C021.MAP @0x25B4), and so is "the Blue Star does not shine there" (@0x5519). Working Designs swapped nothing; the "quiet factual swap" paragraph below is withdrawn.
- **The 1992 魔族 are monsters, not human wrongdoers** (C009.MAP @0x2520; "lived in this world and tormented people," C021.MAP @0x265D). The 1992 Frontier's humans are its villagers. The Dragon Song Japanese 004 #78, in which the Frontier's outlaws "become 魔族" (魔族となって), bridges both readings and is the frame Retold uses.
- **The 500 years and the 封印 exist only on the 1992 Mega CD** (C021.MAP @0x265D, C024.MAP @0x1E7D, C001.MAP @0x4AE7). The 1996 remake deleted every instance and has NPCs believe the 魔族 died out long ago; Working Designs dropped the number from the library line, kept it in two NPC lines, and inserted it into four PS1 lines whose Japanese has none (e.g., Nash, PS1 TEXT022.DAT @0x1B70 vs Saturn TEXT022.DAT @0x1DDC).
- **No Japanese text dates the crossing, Louie, or the magic empire.** Vane has "1,000 years" of magic study (C021.MAP @0x2A13), Althena "over 1,000 years" is Famitsu's press figure, and the Japanese Dragon Song has no year anywhere; "1,000 years before" lives only in Nintendo and Marvelous blurbs.
- **Ubisoft's 006 #5 "received by Black Magic" is JP けいしょうし (inherited):** Ignatius passed the White Dragon's trial honestly. **004 #78 "molded them into a Vile Tribe" is JP 魔族となって (became),** and in the Japanese it is Althena, not Titus, who "felt the danger."
- **Walking School's "people, demons, and demi-humans" is LP commentary,** not game text (the patch dump has no such line). Walking School's own book: Althena reincarnates about once every 500 years and last appeared 200 years before the game (F624 @0x76ECB).
- **Harmony's Four Heroes prologue is in the dump** (`harmony_psp/script_en.txt`, TEXT060.dat; extracted to `harmony_psp/prologue_epilogue_en.txt`), framed as a bedtime story to young Alex and Luna. Secondary canon: Althena's later choice is her answer to Eiphel's one true charge, that she has ruled alone "for too many ages" (TEXT060.dat @0x6A2A).
- **Dyne versus the mad Black Dragon is a 1992 telling** (C029.MAP @0x7BB; C030.MAP @0x3260; C036.MAP @0x22CE) that the 1996 remake dropped and Harmony replaced with the Eiphel battle; cite only 黒竜戦役 from the Lunar 2 book (M0A.GRP @0x1BB20), which is in both versions, and nothing about the war's shape.

The Japanese Silver Star never narrates the creation of Lunar or the crossing from the Blue Star. **[JP]** Japanese Wikipedia's setting entry for Althena says only that "according to legend (伝説によると), she is said to have brought humans to Lunar from the 'Blue Star' that can be seen by looking up from anywhere on Lunar," with no date or method, and its synopsis describes a world "governed by the goddess Althena, the four dragons who support her, and the legendary heroes, the Dragonmasters, who receive her protection," in which "peace that seemed eternal" had continued ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター)). **[JP via fan]** The Mega CD Vane library adds three fragments Kizyr translated: "At the edge of this world stretches the land of the Frontier, where the Blue Star in the heavens does not shine"; "this world was transformed into a land of green through the first Dragonmaster, spoken of in legends"; and "When the Goddess' Tears which covered the world flowed, Vane floated into the sky" ([LP Archive, Differences #4](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2038/)). Kizyr's own gloss, that "Althena was the one who transformed Lunar into a life-harboring place, but the first Dragonmaster and the Four Dragons helped out to some extent," is his commentary pointing forward to Lunar 2, not game text.

**[EN-WD]** Working Designs' PS1 script states Althena's authorship flatly and repeatedly: Jessica's "Althena created our world! Ghaleon could never hold that kind of power!", Damon's Spire riddle "What is the origin of our world, created at the hand of Althena?" (answer: magic), and Laike's "she assumed the form of a human being, to live in the world she created" ([Shotgunnova, SSSC Game Script, via Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20161222072913/http://www.gamefaqs.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs/62187)). **[EN-WD]** Working Designs also made one quiet factual swap: where the Japanese Mega CD ties the Frontier's barrenness to the Blue Star's light not reaching it, the Sega CD English has Vane and Cadin NPCs say "the light from Goddess Althena does not shine there" ([LP Archive, Update 09](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2009/); [Update 26](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2026/)). **[INFERENCE]** That shift matters for a prequel: in the Japanese reading the Blue Star itself is a source of favor or life over Lunar, which Working Designs collapsed into Althena's personal blessing. **Withdrawn 2026-10-10:** the 1992 Japanese has both lines, the goddess's light (C021.MAP @0x25B4) and the Blue Star's (C021.MAP @0x5519); there was no swap.

The how and why of the crossing belong to Eternal Blue, and here the two languages diverge on the single most important fact. **[EN-WD]** Luna's recording in the Goddess Tower says Zophar "came to the Blue Star," fed on "the hatred and the dark desires of the people there," and "succeeded in laying the Blue Star to waste"; Althena sealed him, then "transformed the dead world of Lunar into a thriving land of wonder, and moved the surviving humans there to live in peace, while the Blue Star recovered" ([LP Archive, Lunar: Eternal Blue, Update 47](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2047/)). **[JP]** Japanese Wikipedia's plot summary states the game's late twist in the opposite direction: "Althena's power is 'creation,' and for that it is first necessary to 'destroy'. Its scale is enough to swallow a whole star, and the idea that the Blue Star was once destroyed by Zophar is a mistake (誤り); Althena destroyed the Blue Star in exchange for sealing Zophar" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)). The English script keeps the mechanic in Zophar's mouth ("There can be no new creation without destroying the old. Unleashing Althena's power on me will reduce this world to ash") but never has Luna confess it ([LP Archive, Update 48](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2048/)). **[INFERENCE, withdrawn 2026-10-10; see the correction below]** For a writer honoring the Japanese, Althena arrives on Lunar carrying the guilt of having burned her first world and knowing her own power is as destructive as it is creative; this is the one piece of deep lore that is both Japanese and specific, and it is the natural engine for why she would later doubt whether "one all-powerful being should rule" (the motive Dragon Song gives her, below). The ja.wikipedia summary mixes Mega CD and remake text, so the exact Japanese line should be confirmed from a rip before being quoted.

**Correction (2026-10-09, first-hand).** The Saturn Lunar 2 (JP) discs were ripped (lunar_scripts/l2eb_saturn_jp). The Goddess Tower scenes are voiced movies (O064, Lucia's history; O065, old Luna's recording) with no text anywhere on the discs; both were transcribed with Whisper (three model runs agreeing on the key lines; lunar_scripts/l2eb_saturn_jp/goddess_tower_jp.txt). **[JP]** Lucia: a war against the dark god Zophar, who "preyed on people's desires"; "the Blue Star was wounded and became a land where no one could live" (青き星は傷つき 人の住めない大地へと変わってゆきました); "but we barely managed to seal Zophar" (しかし私たちは かろうじてゾファーを封印することに成功し); "the Blue Star fell into a long, long sleep"; "Althena turned Lunar, a dead star, into a green land and moved there with the few people who survived." **[JP]** Old Luna: "As an absolute god I kept reincarnating in this world, guiding people, and continued to manage the magic to regenerate the Blue Star" (私は絶対の神として この世界で転生を繰り返しながら人々を導き 青き星を再生するために魔法力を管理し続けてきました); "a goddess with absolute power is no use at all for people's true happiness"; "Lunar does not exist only for the Blue Star"; "knowing that, I performed my last reincarnation, to live as one human." Nothing in either movie says Althena destroyed the Blue Star, by her hand or as a price (no 滅ぼした, no 引き換え). **[JP]** The only hint of a cost is the Blue Dragon's cut-off line in the text, 1483 @0xB8E: 「ゾファーを封印するために アルテナの力を使うってのは つまり この世界を……」, silenced by Lucia ("I will not allow that to be spoken of in front of the humans"); and Lemina, after Lucia's account, says "Zophar's power was enough to destroy the Blue Star." **[JP]** The Vane library in Saturn Silver Star Story (TEXT016 @0x932C, @0x97E4) agrees: "once there was a great war on the Blue Star, people hated and wounded one another, and at last the world perished... Althena entrusted the frozen Blue Star to a single girl and, taking the good people with her, came to this world"; "the power that once destroyed the Blue Star... that one is sealed in the darkness." The INFERENCE paragraph above should therefore read: Althena carries the guilt of a world she could not save and a seal that barely held, and her rebirths are tied in her own words to managing Lunar's magic for the Blue Star's regeneration.

**[JP]** Two other Japanese Eternal Blue facts frame the founding era. The official Game Arts blurb for the 2015 PS Archives release calls her 創造の女神アルテナ, the goddess of creation, alongside "the four dragons, the Dragonmaster, and the many battles of the legendary heroes" ([Gamer.ne.jp](https://www.gamer.ne.jp/news/201509160049/)), and Lucia, 青き星の守護者 (guardian of the Blue Star), "had gone to sleep for the restoration of the ruined Blue Star" and possesses "power equal to Althena's" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)). **[JP]** The Blue Spire (青き塔) is "said to be the oldest ruin on Lunar" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)); **[EN-WD]** Lucia says it "has been collecting the magic of your world for many thousands of years" to restore the Blue Star ([LP Archive, Update 46](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2046/)). **[EN-WD]** The Sega CD prologue says "Time and circumstance soon forced Althena to populate Lunar with people from the Blue Star" and that "Thousands of years passed, burying the origin and purpose of the Blue Star" ([LP Archive, Update 01](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2001/)); Gwyn's book says "Once Lunar was a barren world, unable to support life... She blessed it with her power, and it became a place of beauty and wonder. Pleased with her work, she brought people to this land" ([LP Archive, Update 03](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2003/)). Whether the Japanese Mega CD contains that prologue text is unverified.

The most-repeated English version of the founding story is not in any game. **[FAN]** English Wikipedia says the survivors Althena moved "included humans, 'beast-men,' and a race of elf-like magic users," and that "a fourth race" who "rejected Althena's teachings" became the Vile Tribe; the article's plot section has no inline citations and its references point to the 1995 Working Designs Eternal Blue manual and the 1993 Silver Star strategy guide, and a 2007 mirror of the same article said "a third group," showing the race count is an editing artifact ([en.wikipedia Lunar (series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_(series)); [2007 mirror](https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/l/u/n/Lunar_%28series%29.html)). **[INFERENCE]** The "three races came with her, one fell" account is Working Designs manual lineage amplified by wikis. Nothing in the Japanese games found here says who or what besides humans made the crossing.

One date tension deserves flagging because it bears on how old the Dragonmaster line can be. **[JP]** Famitsu's 2009 profile for Harmony, written for Game Arts by original scenario writer Kei Shigema, says Althena "has been at the center of the world for over 1,000 years (1000年以上に渡って)" as of Dyne's era, reincarnating with her memories intact ([Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/sp/091019_lunar/02.html)). **[EN-WD]** Eternal Blue's English prologue and Lucia speak of "thousands of years." **[INFERENCE]** Read literally, the Japanese figure puts Dragon Song's era (about 1,000 years before Alex) near the beginning of Althena's rule on Lunar, which makes a "founding-era" Ignatius chronologically possible but also means the "first Dragonmaster, spoken of in legends" would be only a few generations past at Dragon Song's time. "Over a thousand" is elastic; a rewrite should not build on either number as exact.

## Beastmen are everywhere in Lunar and explained nowhere

No Japanese or English Lunar text found gives beastmen an origin. **[JP]** Japanese sources label individuals 獣人族 and move on: Mel is "a 27-year-old of the beastman tribe" in the Four Heroes era ([Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/sp/091019_lunar/02.html)); Leo, Mauri, and Gwyn are each tagged 獣人族 in Eternal Blue's cast list with no origin anywhere on the page ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)); Senia of Magic School Lunar! is "a tomboyish, lively beastman girl" ([weblio mirror of ja.wikipedia LUNAR](https://www.weblio.jp/content/lunar)). **[JP]** Japanese Wikipedia's list of Mega CD-to-remake changes includes "the beastmen's appearance becomes closer to human" (獣人の姿がより人間に近くなる), so the 1992 original drew them more animal-like ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター)). **[JP]** Nintendo's Japanese product page for Dragon Song describes its world as one where "the two races (二種族) of 'humans' and 'beastmen' live," 1,000 years before ([Nintendo.co.jp ALUJ](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/software/aluj/)).

**[EN-WD]** The English scripts barely say the word. A grep of the full PS1 Silver Star Story Complete script finds no "beastman"; the only racial use is a taunt about Jessica, "life on the line for that beast-chick!" ([Shotgunnova script](http://web.archive.org/web/20161222072913/http://www.gamefaqs.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs/62187)). Across the 61 transcribed updates of the Sega CD Eternal Blue, "beastman" appears only in the LP author's aside ([LP Archive, Update 28](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2028/)). **[EN-WD]** The Sega CD Silver Star's Frontier villages of Cadin, Ruid, and the Talon mine are populated by beastman sprites who are not Vile Tribe ("The life we lead is barely human. We must forage for food and remain on guard against attacks from the Vile Tribe"), which the remake removed ([LP Archive, Update 26](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2026/)). **[FAN]** TV Tropes notes that in Eternal Blue "no one seems to comment on" Gwyn being a beastman and "the game never explains why his grandson, Hiro, is apparently a normal human," and calls Ronfar and Mauri an "Interspecies Romance" ([TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/LunarEternalBlue)). **[FAN]** The Fandom wiki's claim that "In Lunar: Dragon Song, Beastmen were the dominant race... In Silver Star Story, the number of Beastmen had noticeably decreased, and remain minimal in Eternal Blue" is synthesis, not citation ([lunar.fandom.com Lunar](https://lunar.fandom.com/wiki/Lunar)).

**[INFERENCE]** Beastmen are the freest ground in the whole canon. In Japanese Lunar 1 and 2 they are an unremarked, integrated minority: a pirate-turned-governor, a guild archaeologist, a knight-commander and a priestess of the state religion, with no villages of their own, no prejudice scenes found, and no origin dialogue. The Mega CD original's more animal-like look gives a prequel license to draw them less human without contradiction. Dragon Song's own additions (beastmen as the dominant urban class under a Beast King, cat-eared designs, a stated human-beastman tension) are **[EN-other / DS script]** inventions with no counterpart in Lunar 1 or 2, as Hardcore Gaming 101 notes when it calls racial tension the game's main lore addition and remarks on "catgirls here, unlike the elf-like beastmen of the other games" ([HG101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/lunar-dragon-song/)). Nothing official says how beastman society looked a thousand years earlier, so a rewrite can keep Dragon Song's arrangement as a time-specific condition, provided beastmen have faded into a minority by Dyne's day and coexist without comment by Hiro's.

## The Vile Tribe as a race is a 1996 remake decision; the 500-year exile is the only hard number

What both languages of Lunar 1 agree on is small and firm. **[JP via fan]** A Mega CD Vane NPC: "500 years ago, the Vile Tribe, who tormented people, lived in this world. But, the Goddess Althena, for the sake of the people, banished the Vile Tribe to the Frontier and sealed (封印) them there" ([LP Archive, Differences #3](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2037/)). **[EN-WD]** Working Designs kept the exile and the 500 years in both localizations but dropped the sealing in both: the Sega CD NPC says Althena "gathered the Vile Tribe and banished them to the desolation of the Frontier" ([LP Archive, Update 09](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2009/)); the PS1 script has Nash's "banished by Althena 500 years ago," a Talon miner's "since she banished us to the Frontier five hundred years ago," and Phacia's admission, "we were the ones that transgressed the bounds of good" ([Shotgunnova script](http://web.archive.org/web/20161222072913/http://www.gamefaqs.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs/62187)). **[EN-WD]** Xenobia's FMV line, "the innocents Althena callously pushed out beyond the wastelands of the Frontier," has a Working Designs ring and no Japanese confirmation ([Opylex FMV transcript](http://web.archive.org/web/20151019150254/http://www.gamefaqs.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs/48349)).

The decisive Japanese fact is about what the Tribe is. **[JP]** Japanese Wikipedia's list of changes from the Mega CD to the Saturn/PlayStation remake includes "the humans of the Frontier become a separate race called 魔族" (辺境の人間が魔族という別種族になる), and notes that Phacia and Royce "appear only from the ported version and are not in the Mega CD version" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター)). **[JP]** Xenobia's Japanese entry calls her "a woman of the 魔族" who "hates the goddess Althena who drove the 魔族 to the Frontier and burns for revenge on Althena and humans" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター)). **[INFERENCE, corrected 2026-10-10]** The 1992 魔族 are already monsters (horned, winged, black-bodied, counted with 匹, C009.MAP @0x2520); what ja.wikipedia's line describes is the Frontier's villagers, ordinary humans and beastmen in 1992 and gone in 1996. The 1992 Frontier is therefore a sealed monster race living beside a human penal population, which is close to what Dragon Song's Titus describes, and the Japanese 004 #78 (outlaws who "become 魔族") is the bridge.

Lunar 2 removes the Tribe entirely. **[JP]** Japanese Wikipedia's Eternal Blue article never uses 魔族; its villains are the human theocracy アルテナ神団 and Borgan, "a man who originally had no magical power at all" who built Neo-Vane with the Black Dragon's borrowed power ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)). **[EN-WD]** The words "Vile" and "Frontier" do not occur in any transcribed Sega CD Eternal Blue dialogue ([LP Archive index](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/)). **[FAN]** A forum essay appended to that LP reports that the Japanese side material (Magic School Lunar! and the post-SSSC manga) has the Tribe interbreeding into humans and beastmen, with Phacia pushing integration after Xenobia's death, so that by Eternal Blue "they've been fully bred in" ([LP Archive, Update 61](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2061/)). **[EN-other]** The Aeon Genesis fan translation of Walking School, set "hundreds of years earlier" than Silver Star ([en.wikipedia Lunar: Sanposuru Gakuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar:_Sanposuru_Gakuen)), contains **no** account of the Tribe's composition or origin. An earlier draft of this report quoted "The Vile Tribe is a group of people, demons, and demi-humans... Althena banished them to a desolate land called the Frontier" from the LP Archive, but a dump of every string in the translation patch (lunar_scripts/walking_school_gg, 2,074 strings; tools/lunar_scripts/ips_text.py) shows that passage is the Let's Play author's own commentary, not game text: the script never contains "demi", "banish", or "Frontier". What the game does say: Memphis is "the lord of the demon castle, D" and is called Vile Tribe; "the Goddess tends to appear as a human once every 500 years... she incarnated 200 years ago"; Althena "has incarnated in Iyen"; one "beastmen" line; the Blue Dragon visits the island "once every ten years" as a guardian of time. Corrected 2026-10-09.

The rest of what circulates is uncited. **[FAN]** The Fandom wiki's Vile Tribe page says their "origins are uncertain. Some sources describe them as 'magic made flesh'... Others suggest a link to an ancient Magic Empire," that they were "once an integral part of Lunar's magical society," and that Ghaleon "was the last known member of the Tribe still residing in" Vane ([lunar.fandom.com Vile Tribe](https://lunar.fandom.com/wiki/Vile_Tribe)); none of it appears in either Lunar 1 script found. **[JP]** The closest Japanese statement about Ghaleon's nature is Famitsu's Harmony profile: he belongs to a 長命種族 (long-lived race), has lived several times longer than he looks, and "for a time saw humans only as a bystander" until Dyne showed him their potential ([Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/sp/091019_lunar/02.html)). No Japanese source found calls him 魔族. **[JP]** Zophar, 破壊神 (god of destruction), is never connected to the Vile Tribe in any text; his only creations in Eternal Blue are corruptions of existing people ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)). **[INFERENCE]** A prequel wanting to tie the Tribe to Zophar or to the Blue Star would be adding to the canon, not drawing from it.

## The Dragonmaster line has legendary predecessors but no founder who crossed with Althena

The Japanese definition of the office is institutional, not genealogical. **[JP]** A Dragonmaster is "guardian of the goddess Althena and representative of all life. Only one who has passed the trials of the four dragons, white, blue, black and red, gains the title," bearing Althena's Sword, the Black Dragon Armor, the Red Dragon Shield, the Blue Dragon Helm, and the White Dragon Wings ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター)). **[JP]** Dyne is 先代のドラゴンマスター, the previous Dragonmaster, and "after him no hero was born"; the series page says "with the previous Dyne as the last, no warrior calling himself Dragonmaster was born" (先代のダインを最後にドラゴンマスターを名乗る戦士も生まれなくなっていた), and Alex is 最後のドラゴンマスター, the last ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター); [weblio mirror of ja.wikipedia LUNAR](https://www.weblio.jp/content/lunar)). The word 先代 presupposes predecessors. **[JP]** Eternal Blue's prologue opens "a long time after the adventures of the last Dragonmaster, Alex," and its theology is explicit: no goddess, no Dragonmaster, so Hiro ends the game with Althena's Sword but never the title, and the revived Ghaleon is only a "(fake) Dragonmaster" under Zophar ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)).

Earlier Dragonmasters are attested but never dated. **[JP via fan]** The Mega CD library book credits "the first Dragonmaster, spoken of in legends" with turning the world green ([LP Archive, Differences #4](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2038/)). **[EN-WD]** The PS1 Blue Dragon says "Alicia and Laticia always joked that I should be called the Blabbing Blue Dragon," and Nall identifies them as "names of ancient Dragonmasters" ([Shotgunnova script](http://web.archive.org/web/20161222072913/http://www.gamefaqs.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs/62187)). **[EN-WD]** The Sega CD Eternal Blue Vane library names "the most popular Dragonmasters in history" as "'Cyclone' Zeon, 'Iron Fist' Loka, Dyne, and the last Dragonmaster... Alex" ([LP Archive, Lunar: Eternal Blue, Update 25](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Eternal-Blue/Update%2025/)). Whether any of these four names translate Japanese library text or are Working Designs inventions is unknown; Working Designs did rename Fidy to Quark and Honmel to Caldor in the same game ([LP Archive, Differences #1](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2035/)).

The founder-who-came-with-Althena story has exactly one source. **[FAN]** The Fandom wiki's Dragonmaster page lists "Louie, the first Dragonmaster who accompanied Althena when she travelled from the Blue Star to Lunar; Ignatius...; Jian...; Leon, the Mighty Gale; Gaull, the Iron Hand; Alicia and Laticia...; Lady Chloe, a beastwoman...; Nike...; Natto, the Singing Swordsman; Dyne...; Alex Noa," and its Blue Star page asserts "nine Dragon Masters"; no citations are given, the page returned HTTP 402 to every fetch, and no second source confirms Louie ([lunar.fandom.com Dragonmaster](https://lunar.fandom.com/wiki/Dragonmaster); [lunar.fandom.com Blue Star](https://lunar.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Star)). **[INFERENCE]** "Leon, the Mighty Gale" and "Gaull, the Iron Hand" look like the Sega CD's Zeon and Loka under a later rendering, Chloe is from Magic School Lunar!, and Ignatius and Jian are Dragon Song's. The Fandom wiki's Four Dragons page likewise says Althena "created four divine Dragons" after transporting the survivors and "designated a Dragonmaster," again uncited ([lunar.fandom.com Four Dragons](https://lunar.fandom.com/wiki/Four_Dragons)). **[JP]** Official Japanese text found describes the Dragonmaster only as "the legendary warrior who receives the goddess's protection" and never names a first one ([weblio mirror](https://www.weblio.jp/content/lunar)).

Game Arts' own 2009 addition to the past shows how the rights-holder writes this material. **[JP]** Harmony's prologue, written by Kei Shigema, is a playable final battle of the Four Heroes at the undersea fortress リバイアサン against a masked human sorcerer アイフェルン and the 黒き星の五王子 (Five Princes of the Black Star); Aifeln, about 24, "once travelled with Dyne and Althena as an ordinary man," then broke with them "over the nature of the world and the gods," has "an exceedingly human, weak heart," and tries to become a ruling god by taking Althena's power; Dyne is "a magic swordsman who commands the four dragons" ([Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/sp/091019_lunar/02.html); [Inside Games](https://www.inside-games.jp/article/2009/11/04/38582.html)). **[EN-other]** The English version has Eiphel prophesy "that Ghaleon will fall the same way he did" ([The Rat's Den](https://laurietom.blogspot.com/2011/09/rpg-talk-lunar-silver-star-harmony.html)). **[INFERENCE]** The prologue says nothing about the creation, the migration, beastmen, the Vile Tribe, or any pre-Dyne Dragonmaster, and it ignores Dragon Song entirely; what it supplies is a template, a companion of goddess and Dragonmaster who turns over the question of whether the world needs a god.

## Ignatius fits a later Dragonmaster far better than a founder

Dragon Song fixes little about Ignatius, which is why both backstories are technically open. **[DS script]** The White Dragon says "He took my trial, received by Black Magic, and went on to become a Dragonmaster" (006 #5); Titus says Althena came to doubt that one all-powerful being should rule and wanted the future "entrusted... to all the people in the world" (004 #78), while Ignatius "believes the opposite" and "desires to take the seat of Goddess Althena" (004 #80); the Frontier "existed to confine those evildoers for whom there was no other hope" until Ignatius molded them into an army (004 #78); he tells Jian that giving up the rings changes nothing because he passed the trials (021 #26); he spares Jian at Althena's word ("As you wish, Goddess!", 021 #12), and dies by falling, refusing Jian's hand (021 #38). In Japanese he is イグナティウス, speaks with the royal 余 and archaic なり, and his signature line あいは おしみなく うばう quotes the title of Arishima Takeo's 1920 essay 惜みなく愛は奪う (021 #24) (`docs/story-characters.md`, sections 6.1 to 6.5). **[FAN]** English Wikipedia calls him "a member of the Vile Tribe and an expert magician who covets the power of the Goddess" and the Fandom wiki "an evil Dragonmaster from the Vile Tribe" ([en.wikipedia Lunar: Dragon Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar:_Dragon_Song); [lunar.fandom.com Dragonmaster](https://lunar.fandom.com/wiki/Dragonmaster)); the script itself has him lead the Tribe, not belong to it by birth. No Japanese guidebook, interview, or official page giving him any past beyond the game was found; the June 2005 Weekly Famitsu feature introduced only Jian, Lucia, and Gabryel ([RPGFan 2005-06-11](https://classic.rpgfan.com/news/2005/1580.html)).

**Option A, the Dragonmaster who came with Althena from the Blue Star.** What it has going for it: **[JP]** Harmony's "over 1,000 years" of rule puts Dragon Song near the founding, so a founder could still be alive if he is long-lived (Ghaleon's 長命種族 is Japanese precedent for a non-human lifespan), and **[JP via fan]** the Mega CD's "first Dragonmaster, spoken of in legends" who greened the world is a Japanese hook that a fallen founder could darkly invert. What it costs: **[JP]** no Japanese source has any Dragonmaster, or anyone but humans, cross with Althena, and the Japanese Lunar 1 keeps the whole crossing as 伝説; **[JP]** Lunar 2's Japanese account makes the crossing inseparable from the ruin of the Blue Star and the barely-held sealing of Zophar (see the correction in the first section), so a founder-companion would have to know and carry that secret, importing Eternal Blue's entire cosmology into a game that otherwise never mentions Zophar or Lucia of the Blue Star; **[DS script]** Dragon Song's own White Dragon says Ignatius "took my trial," which presupposes dragons and trials that existed before him, and a founder who came with the goddess would not be a trial-taker but the man for whom trials were instituted; **[EN-WD]** the Sega CD Eternal Blue's "thousands of years" since the migration, if kept, makes a founder alive at minus 1,000 a multi-millennial immortal, a scale the series reserves for gods and dragons; and **[FAN]** the only existing "first Dragonmaster who came with Althena" is Louie, so the rewrite would be either adopting an uncited wiki figure or contradicting it. **[INFERENCE]** Option A contradicts no Japanese sentence outright, because the Japanese is silent, but it forces the writer to fill the silence Game Arts chose to keep, and it collides with Dragon Song's own text.

**Option B, a Dragonmaster later in an already-old line.** **[JP]** 先代 for Dyne, a first Dragonmaster "spoken of in legends," and Harmony's "over 1,000 years" of rule all presuppose a line with several holders before Dyne; **[EN-WD]** Alicia and Laticia, Zeon and Loka are attested predecessors with no dates, so a Dragonmaster at minus 1,000 slots between the legendary first and those names without displacing anyone; **[JP]** Lunar 1's dragons remember only Dyne, which is consistent if Ignatius's tenure ends with his death and the dragons' allegiance passes to Jian, and Eternal Blue's rule that the title cannot exist without the goddess is untouched. **[JP]** Game Arts' own Aifeln, a former companion of Althena and the Dragonmaster who turned on them over the nature of gods and tried to seize her power, is the closest thing to an official template for a Lunar prequel villain, and it is Shigema's. **[INFERENCE]** Option B contradicts the least by a wide margin. Its one cost is giving up the grandeur of the founding, which can be recovered by making Ignatius the bearer of founding-era memory (a long-lived man who heard the first Dragonmaster's story, or who was that Dragonmaster's pupil) rather than the founder himself.

**[INFERENCE]** A middle path exists and is cheap: Ignatius as the Dragonmaster who passed the trials in the ordinary way long ago, in the age when the world was still being greened, who outlived his human span by Black Magic and whose fall is remembered by Lunar 1 only as the sealing of the Frontier. That keeps the Japanese "sealed" (封印) detail, uses the 1992 original's human Vile Tribe, and leaves "the first Dragonmaster" where the Japanese library left him: in legend.

## Dragon Song's real contradictions are Althena's abdication and the Vile Tribe's age

The large one is theological. **[DS script]** Dragon Song has Althena conclude that the future should belong to all people, tell the party "Each of you are already... just as powerful as I ever could be" (021 #32), release her power, and be reborn as Lucia, a thousand years before Alex (`docs/story-characters.md`, sections 4 and 6.4). **[JP]** Japanese Lunar 1 has her active as goddess in Dyne's day, ruling "for over 1,000 years" with memories intact ([Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/sp/091019_lunar/02.html)), and makes her decision to become human a single event fifteen years before the game: "Fifteen years ago, the goddess Althena, who had come to share hearts with Dyne, decided herself to reincarnate as a 'human,' and through that ritual Dyne lost his power as Dragonmaster" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ザ・シルバースター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ザ・シルバースター)); Japanese Lunar 2 calls Luna "the woman the goddess Althena last reincarnated as" and says the heroes "ended the age of gods completely" ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)). **[JP via fan]** The Mega CD does say Althena "resides in the Goddess Tower at the heart of the world, continuing her rebirths" ([LP Archive, Differences #4](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2038/)), and **[EN-WD]** a Sega CD NPC says "In times past, she has even come to live among the people as a human!" ([LP Archive, Update 09](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2009/)). **[INFERENCE]** So an Althena who withdraws and is reborn as a human at minus 1,000 is canon-compatible only if it is one of her periodic rebirths with memory kept, not the permanent surrender of divinity that Japanese Lunar 1 assigns to Dyne's era and Japanese Lunar 2 declares final. The rewrite should let her reach the doubt and act on it partially; her long absence in Lunar 1 ("at some point the goddess Althena stopped showing herself," いつの頃からか女神アルテナはその姿を現さなくなり, [weblio mirror](https://www.weblio.jp/content/lunar)) is an unfilled gap that Dragon Song can be the beginning of. A blogger reached the same conclusion from the English side: "Althena is still around when Alex begins his Dragonmaster journey," and Vane's levitation "would have required Althena to still be Althena, not Lucia" ([dcgameblog](https://www.dcgameblog.com/2024/04/lunar-retrospective-dragon-song/)).

The second is chronological. **[JP via fan]** Lunar 1 dates the Vile Tribe's exile and sealing to 500 years before Alex ([LP Archive, Differences #3](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2037/)); **[EN-other]** Walking School, "hundreds of years" before Alex, already has them exiled ([LP Archive, Walking School, Update 10](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Walking-School/Update%2010/)); **[DS script]** Dragon Song, 1,000 years before Alex, has a Vile Tribe nation in the Frontier whose people say "even the Goddess Althena, the very one who sent us here, has long since forgotten that we exist" (010 #15), and credits Ignatius with molding the Frontier's confined evildoers into that people. **[INFERENCE]** Taken literally, Dragon Song's Tribe exists 500 years before Lunar 1 says it was created by exile, and Ignatius as its maker contradicts the Japanese Vane NPC's account of a tribe that "tormented people" and was then banished by Althena. The least-cost fix is to make Dragon Song's Frontier population the penal colony Titus describes (which matches the 1992 original's human Frontier) and leave the 魔族-as-a-people and their formal sealing to a later, unseen event, so that Lunar 1's 500-year memory is of the second, larger banishment rather than of this one.

The remaining strains are softer. **[DS script]** Two living Dragonmasters at once is unusual, which Dragon Song's English explains by Ignatius's trial "by Black Magic" (006 #5; the Japanese says he inherited, けいしょうし, the dragon's magic, so he passed honestly); a blogger notes Silver Star "suggested Dragonmasters rise only to fill a void" ([dcgameblog](https://www.dcgameblog.com/2024/04/lunar-retrospective-dragon-song/)). **[FAN]** English Wikipedia's note that the four dragons "are replaced over time... they sleep underground until needed" is the handle for reconciling dragons consumed in Dragon Song's trials with four living dragons in Alex's day ([en.wikipedia Lunar (series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_(series))). **[EN-other / FAN]** The Cathedral and Chamber of Rebirth vanishing, and Vane's absence (early designs included Vane but it was cut, per HG101), are gaps rather than contradictions ([HG101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/lunar-dragon-song/)). **[JP]** A name collision deserves a note: Dragon Song's heroine is ルシア・コリンズ ([RPGFan 2005-06-11](https://classic.rpgfan.com/news/2005/1580.html)) while Eternal Blue's Blue Star guardian is ルーシア ([ja.wikipedia LUNAR ETERNAL BLUE](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUNAR_ETERNAL_BLUE)); both are "Lucia" in English, and Dragon Song's ending makes its Lucia the reborn Althena, which a Lunar 2 reader will hear as a claim about the other Lucia. **[INFERENCE]** The rewrite should either lean into the echo deliberately or avoid lines that make Dragon Song's Lucia sound like Lunar 2's.

On canon status, the signal is silence. **[JP]** Game Arts holds the copyright line ("©1992 GAME ARTS ©2005 GAME ARTS / Japan Art Media / Marvelous Interactive Inc.", [Nintendo.co.jp ALUJ](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/software/aluj/)), but Studio Alex, which wrote Lunar 1 and 2, had gone bankrupt in 2003 and "had no part in it," Kubooka returned only as design supervisor, and the game was "a fallback after a planned Lunar 3 was abandoned" ([HG101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/lunar-dragon-song/); [en.wikipedia Lunar: Dragon Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar:_Dragon_Song)). **[JP]** Every later Game Arts or GungHo Lunar product, Harmony in 2009 and the 2025 Remastered Collection, omits it ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2024/10/14/145981.html)). **[INFERENCE]** The user's stance, that Japanese Lunar 1 and 2 are canon and Dragon Song yields where it conflicts, is how the rights-holder has behaved.

## Three English scripts download; every Japanese script must be ripped (history: all ripped by 2026-10-09, see `lunar_scripts/README.md`)

The research found no transcript of any Lunar game in Japanese after a dozen Japanese-language searches ("セリフ集", "台詞集", "全テキスト", "名言集"); the results were strategy sites only ([rrpg.jp](https://rrpg.jp/lunar/); [rpgdl](https://www.rpgdl.org/ss/lunarsss.html)). Every Japanese claim in this report therefore rests on Japanese Wikipedia, Famitsu, Nintendo, Game Watch, or a fan translator's rendering, and the Japanese side of the sourcing table below is "rip" in every row. Kizyr's LunarNET, the only systematic Japanese-English difference list ever compiled, was offline ("Temporarily closed for maintenance") throughout, and its content survives only in the five "Differences" posts of the Sega CD Let's Play ([LP Archive, Update 35](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star/Update%2035/)).

On the English side, three full scripts exist. **[EN-WD]** Shotgunnova's GameFAQs Game Scripts for Silver Star Story Complete (346 KB, 2011) and Eternal Blue Complete (372 KB, 2008) are the only complete transcripts of any Lunar game online; GameFAQs returns 403 to automated fetches, so they must be opened in a browser or read through Wayback snapshots ([SSSC listing](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs); [SSSC script via Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20161222072913/http://www.gamefaqs.com/ps/197808-lunar-silver-star-story-complete/faqs/62187); [EBC listing](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps/197807-lunar-2-eternal-blue/faqs)). **[EN-WD]** The Sega CD Eternal Blue text is a git-cloneable dump, studio-lucia/lunar_eb_script, 80 files `M00.GRP.txt` to `M4F.GRP.txt` with `[POR02]`, `[BRK]`, `[END]` control tags, though it is the "Un-Worked Design" edited text rather than pristine Working Designs ([lunar_eb_script](https://github.com/studio-lucia/lunar_eb_script)). The Sega CD Silver Star English has no transcript at all beyond the screenshot LP ([LP Archive](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-The-Silver-Star)). Legends of Localization's one Lunar article confirms the Working Designs PS1 Tootsie Pop and M&M's lines are absent from the Saturn Japanese ([Legends of Localization](https://legendsoflocalization.com/articles/lunar-silver-star-references/)), and RPGamer reported in 1998 that "only 15% of the original script" survived into the PlayStation version ([RPGamer](https://archive.rpgamer.com/news/Q4-1998/102098a.html)).

The ripping tools cluster in three repositories. Supper's wdtools (GPL-3) covers both Sega CD games: `scriptrip_tss` and `lunardat` for The Silver Star, `scriptrip` (English) and `scriptrip_jp` (Japanese, with `src/scriptrip_jp_thingy.txt` for Unicode conversion) for Eternal Blue, plus `lsss_extr`/`lsss_txt` for the PS1 SSSC and `l2eb_data`/`l2eb_txt` for the PS1 EBC, and generic `sjis_srch` and `tablerip`; the author warns "most tools are one-offs with hardcoded offsets" ([wdtools](https://github.com/suppertails66/wdtools)). MrConan1's Lunar Script Builder (`lsb`) decodes and re-encodes `TEXTxxx.DAT` for Saturn Silver Star Story, the Saturn MPEG "Complete," PS1, and iOS, in 2-byte mode for Japanese and BPE-compressed 1-byte mode for English, with `lsb.exe decode In Out ienc [sss]`; the same author's lunar2_eb_sat_tools handles the Saturn Lunar 2 ([lsb README](https://github.com/MrConan1/lsb/blob/master/README.md); [MrConan1 repos](https://github.com/MrConan1?tab=repositories)). Misty De Méo's studio-lucia organization holds sssunpack for the Saturn MPEG edition's packed `.dat` containers, sss_script_tools (Ruby `script_printer` for Saturn Japanese control codes), format notes in lunardata (Saturn 1996, Saturn Complete 1997, PS1 JP and US, Windows 1999, iOS 2012) and eternaldata (Saturn 1998, PS1 1999 and 2000), and for Magic School Lunar! the documented `msl_script_dump file.fld`, which writes one CSV per `.fld` with Shift JIS converted to UTF-8, after unpacking with fldtools ([studio-lucia org](https://github.com/orgs/studio-lucia/repositories); [msl_script_tools](https://github.com/studio-lucia/msl_script_tools); [lunardata](https://github.com/studio-lucia/lunardata); [eternaldata](https://github.com/studio-lucia/eternaldata)). **[INFERENCE]** For the Silver Star Story and Eternal Blue Complete Japanese-English pair, the cheapest route is Shotgunnova's English plus an `lsb` or `lunar2_eb_sat_tools` dump of the Japanese Saturn or PS1 disc; for the Sega CD pair, wdtools gives both languages of Eternal Blue directly.

Three games have no published tooling. Lunar Legend (GBA) has no table, dumper, or format notes on romhacking.net, GitHub, or TCRF; the fact that Ubisoft's text was hex-edited in place with truncations suggests uncompressed fixed-width text, so a relative-search `.tbl` and a table dumper is the route ([en.wikipedia Lunar Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Legend)). Silver Star Harmony (PSP) stores text in ScriptPack `.dat` containers holding `TEXTxxx.dat` files; a GBAtemp attempt extracted `TEXT000.dat` but could not read it, and the undub, Italian, and Spanish patches prove the text is editable without publishing their tools ([GBAtemp thread](https://gbatemp.net/threads/finding-game-script-for-silver-star-harmony.525000/); [romhacking 4060](https://www.romhacking.net/hacks/4060/)). **[INFERENCE]** Because the inner file naming matches the `lsb`-supported `TEXTxxx.DAT` family, an sssunpack-style container reader followed by `lsb decode` with the PSX or iOS table is the first thing to try; the Japanese Harmony prologue is the single most valuable text to rip, since it is Game Arts-authored and exists nowhere in print. Walking School (Game Gear) has Aeon Genesis's complete English patch (v1.00, 2009-12-17) and a 21-update screenshot LP, but no table or dump in either language ([Aeon Genesis](https://aeongenesis.net/projects/lunar); [LP Archive](https://lparchive.org/Lunar-Walking-School/)). Magic School Lunar!'s English fan translation is "entering the late phases" with its script in private CSV files; its author is the person to ask for the Japanese CSVs ([Saturn Shiro, 2026-04-01](https://www.segasaturnshiro.com/2026/04/01/script-editing-tool-aids-magic-school-lunar-fan-translation/); [msl-script-editor](https://codeberg.org/mistydemeo/msl-script-editor)). Dragon Song is already ripped in this repository: `uv run python -m dsde.text_dump` writes USA and Japanese side by side per message op to `build/text/script_NNN.txt` (`docs/re-japanese.md`). The 2025 Lunar Remastered Collection uses the Working Designs English "word-for-word" minus offensive material and 1990s references, with both languages in one package but no documented archive format ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2025/04/lunar-remastered-collection-uses-the-working-designs-script)).

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual assumption about Lunar's lore. The elaborate founding story that English fans repeat, with three races crossing from the Blue Star, a fourth falling, and a named first Dragonmaster at Althena's side, is a Working Designs manual-and-wiki construction; Game Arts' Japanese games say less and, where they say anything, say something sparser (a Blue Star wounded and barely sealed, not burned; a 1992 Vile Tribe of monsters sealed beside a human penal Frontier; three dated events in all; corrected 2026-10-10). A rewrite that wants the Japanese creators' vision is therefore not constrained by a dense canon but by a sparse one, and its discipline should be to invent in the silences rather than to overwrite the few hard facts: the 500-year exile and sealing, Dyne as 先代, Althena active and divine until fifteen years before Alex, and the title dying with the goddess.

The practical consequence is that the one decision the user asked about resolves cleanly while the one they did not ask about is the real problem. Ignatius as a later Dragonmaster is nearly free; Althena's abdication and rebirth as Lucia is Dragon Song's genuine break with both Japanese games and will need to become a partial, remembered withdrawal rather than an ending. Everything in this report that rests on **[JP via fan]** or on Japanese Wikipedia paraphrase should be verified against a rip before it is quoted in game text; the tools to do that exist for the two games that matter most.

## Recommendations for the Ignatius backstory

Make Ignatius a Dragonmaster later in the line, not the founder. Every Japanese source presupposes predecessors before Dyne and names none, so a Dragonmaster at minus 1,000 displaces nothing, while Dragon Song's own "he took my trial" line already places dragons and trials before him. Use Game Arts' Aifeln as the model: a former companion of Althena and of the goddess's chosen champion who breaks with her over whether the world needs a god, and who tries to take her power because, in his reading, she has abandoned her duty by doubting her own rule. That is Shigema's template, and Ignatius's Japanese voice (余, なり, the Arishima quotation) already reads as a lettered old aristocrat who believes he is defending order.

If the founding era must be present, give it to Ignatius as memory rather than identity: let him be long-lived (Japanese precedent exists in Ghaleon's 長命種族), and let him have known or served "the first Dragonmaster, spoken of in legends" (do not say who greened the world; the 1992 and 1996 texts disagree). That keeps the Mega CD's one Japanese line about a first Dragonmaster intact as legend, which is exactly how Lunar 1 treats it, and avoids importing Lunar 2's Zophar and Blue Star cosmology into a game that never mentions them. **Superseded 2026-10-10:** Louie is canon (M0A.GRP @0x1BA78), so he may be named; the bible's 11a already does.

Fix the Vile Tribe's age at the same time, because Ignatius's role as their maker is where Dragon Song contradicts Lunar 1 most directly. Keep Titus's penal-colony Frontier (it matches the 1992 original's human Frontier better than the 1996 remake's separate race) and frame Ignatius's army as a first gathering that Althena will later, offstage and some 500 years before Alex, banish and seal as a people. Keep the Japanese detail that she sealed (封印) them, which Working Designs dropped. And give Althena's ending a Japanese-compatible shape: she withdraws and is reborn with her memory kept, as the Mega CD library says she has always done, so that Lunar 1's "at some point the goddess stopped showing herself" begins here, and her final, permanent choice remains Dyne's and Luna's. Before any of this goes into game text, rip the Japanese Eternal Blue Goddess Tower scene (the Blue Star destruction line), the Japanese Vane library books in both Silver Star versions, and the Japanese Harmony prologue; those three rips would convert most of this report's **[JP via fan]** and paraphrase tags to **[JP]**.
